1 · Getting started

  1. Open d3 Sight Reading Trainer (d3SRT) in Chrome or another Web MIDI-capable browser, served over HTTPS.
  2. Connect a USB MIDI keyboard and select it in Cosmic Harmony · MIDI bridge. A keyboard that only outputs analog audio will not supply MIDI notes.
  3. Click Open Cosmic Harmony; place d3SRT and Cosmic Harmony side by side or on separate screens.
  4. In Cosmic Harmony, choose Major, Minor or Blues and select the Sun/key. Select Guided harmonic journey or Choose your own route.
  5. Play the opening tonic on your keyboard. Play the indicated target chord or any permitted green-outlined chord in Free Route.
  6. After completing a challenge, click Send to staff. In d3SRT set a comfortable BPM, enable Loop, press Play, and practice with the repeating accompaniment.

Practice idea: First play only the chord roots in your left hand, then walk a bass line, then use third-and-seventh shells or a rhythmic right-hand comping pattern. Repeat at progressively higher tempos.

2 · Guided and free journeys

Guided harmonic journey

Select a journey family and bar length. The software gives you a target chord and harmonic explanation. You play the actual MIDI voicing; the Journey Replay list records your steps and allows audition.

Choose your own route

After the opening tonic, the green dotted outlines show currently permitted moves. Play a permitted chord directly—no mouse click required. Visit the selected destination planet, then eventually play the Sun tonic. Use Finish Journey when you're satisfied; you may keep playing and explore additional resolutions before finishing. End Journey stops an incomplete exercise.

Free Exploration / MIDI Auto-trace

Check Free Exploration · MIDI Auto-trace to trace permitted chords without a Challenge. Use Send free path to staff to make a staff accompaniment. Saved paths can also be sent to staff, with generated voicings when the original MIDI notes weren't preserved.

Repeat a progression

Loop a sent take in d3SRT as many times as you like. The harmonic path and a particular MIDI performance are distinct concepts; new takes can explore different voicings and groove choices.

3 · Recording, staff, and loop transport

Record captures incoming MIDI. Play/Pause alternates playback and pausing at the current position. Stop ends playback and resets. Loop repeats the current take continuously. Clear clears the current staff take without affecting saved journeys or exported files.

Change BPM to slow a difficult passage or speed up a groove. During playback, staff positions and Cosmic nodes/paths highlight together. Clicking a Journey Replay step locates the corresponding measure. Repeated chords are tracked by journey step—not by chord label alone.

Recording tools includes JSON take import/export and visual notation cleanup. Quantization in that panel changes the notation preview, not the original recorded timing. MIDI and notation aren't identical: a sent Challenge typically displays one chord per 4/4 bar, retaining actual note pitches but spacing chords for practice.

Ways to use the loop: play the full chords in either hand, practice smooth voice leading, play walking bass with the left hand, improvise melodies, add percussive offbeats or a reggae feel, or pick up a guitar and solo over the same changes.

4 · Read the Cosmic map

The Sun represents the home tonic. Planets are diatonic chord functions. Close moons suggest dominant approaches to a destination; distant moons represent ii approaches that can lead through a V to the destination. An arrow traces the chosen harmonic movement. A dotted outline indicates a currently permitted next choice.

In a major key, a familiar homecoming is ii–V–I (for example Dm7 → G7 → Cmaj7). In minor, the corresponding homecoming often uses iiø–V–i (for example Dm7♭5 → G7 → Cm). The visual design and navigation rules support exploration; they do not imply that geometric distance measures harmonic distance.

Identical chords at different nodes: a chord such as Em7 may be both a planet and a moon in B minor. MIDI cannot distinguish harmonic function from identical note sets, so Free Route uses context and favors a permitted planet when equivalent choices exist.

Source and adaptation: Major and minor harmonic astronomy are inspired by Randy Halberstadt's Metaphors for the Musician, Chapter 22. This interactive implementation is an adaptation; its generated journeys, MIDI recognition, playback, and exploratory Blues mode are software extensions, not features or rules claimed to be in the original book.

5 · Troubleshooting

No MIDI response?

Connect the keyboard in d3SRT first, check the chosen MIDI input, and confirm that both pages are from the same GitHub Pages origin. In Cosmic Harmony open Standalone MIDI / diagnostics to check Held keys, recognition, and raw events. Release sustain if notes seem stuck; use Clear held notes if needed.

Chord isn't accepted?

Check the outlined permitted nodes and the chord prompt. In Jazz mode, complete seventh chords or root–third–seventh shells often work best. Triads may be accepted for some chord types; rootless or ambiguous note groups can still require a clearer voicing. The same sounding chord can occupy more than one harmonic role.

The map doesn't follow playback?

Send the current Challenge or Free Exploration path to staff first. Keep both windows open and on the same site origin. Ensure the staff contains the corresponding journey take; a different imported or recorded take won't necessarily have journey-step metadata.

I lost a saved path or recording

Named paths can be exported as JSON from Cosmic Harmony's Saved and archived journeys section. Export recordings separately from d3SRT's Recording tools. Browser-local state and unsaved recordings aren't a substitute for exported backups.

6 · The philosophy behind the project

The development method is practical and iterative: build something useful → use it in real life → observe what's missing → improve it. The same principle guided the developer's van build: the design evolved through actual use rather than being prescribed all at once.

What began as a sight-reading trainer and a visualization of harmonic relationships became more than a looper: it records a performance, connects it to a harmonic journey, shows staff notation and musical function simultaneously, and supports repeated study from different angles. The harmonic composition can remain conceptually separate from any one performance, creating a foundation for later arranging, transposition, and multiple-take comparison.

The educational aim is not to replace disciplined practice but to make practice intrinsically engaging. A looping journey can become an exercise in chord memory, timing, walking bass, voicings, improvisation, or composition simply by changing how you approach it. Learning itself becomes the game—the reward is making music, not collecting points.
